Let's get into it

Your skin has a built-in bestie.

The skin barrier is the outermost part of your skin. It helps keep water in and protect your body from outside irritants. When it is not functioning well, skin may feel dry, tight, flaky, or sensitive.

💧

Helps hold in moisture

A healthy skin barrier helps reduce water loss and supports skin that feels soft and comfortable.

🛡️

Helps keep irritants out

The outer skin layer acts as a protective barrier against many environmental irritants.

🌵

Dry weather can be a factor

Low humidity, hot showers, harsh cleansers, and overusing exfoliating products may contribute to dryness or irritation.

Signs to notice

When your skin wants a reset.

  • Skin feels tight after washing.
  • Dry patches or flaking appear.
  • Products that usually feel fine start to sting.
  • Your skin feels unusually rough or uncomfortable.
These signs can have different causes and do not confirm that your skin barrier is damaged. Persistent or severe symptoms deserve advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
The routine reset

Less drama. More consistency.

You do not need to add five new products at once. Start with a gentle cleanser, a moisturizer that suits your skin, and daytime sunscreen.

Consider pausing exfoliating acids, scrubs, or other potentially irritating products if your skin is feeling sensitive. Reintroduce products gradually once your skin is comfortable again.

Your simple routine

Three steps. No overthinking.

A basic routine can help you avoid adding unnecessary irritation while supporting your skin's natural barrier.

  1. Cleanse without stripping.

    Choose a gentle cleanser and use lukewarm rather than very hot water. Avoid scrubbing your skin.

  2. Moisturize consistently.

    Look for a moisturizer that suits your skin. Ingredients such as glycerin, ceramides, and petrolatum may help support hydration and reduce moisture loss.

  3. Protect your skin during the day.

    Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher, follow its directions, and combine it with shade and protective clothing.

Frequently asked questions

Barrier care, decoded.

How long does it take for a skin barrier to recover?

It depends on what is causing the irritation and how severe it is. Mild dryness may improve with a gentler routine, while ongoing irritation may need professional advice.

Should I stop using all my skin care products?

Not necessarily. Simplifying your routine can help you identify potential irritants. Focus on gentle cleansing, moisturizing, and sun protection, and pause products that seem to worsen symptoms.

Are fragrance-free products better?

Fragrance-free products may be a useful option if your skin is sensitive or easily irritated. However, no single label guarantees that a product will work for everyone.
