The desert hydration guide

Moisturizer for Dry Skin in Summer

Hot weather does not always mean hydrated skin. Dry air, strong sunlight, sweat, and constant air conditioning can leave your face feeling tight even when temperatures are high. The right summer moisturizer helps keep skin comfortable without making your routine feel heavy.

Build a summer routine

Why summer skin gets dry

Heat outside. Dry air inside.

Living in a desert climate can mean moving between hot outdoor air and cool, air-conditioned spaces throughout the day. Low humidity can contribute to moisture loss, while frequent washing and strong skincare products can make dryness feel worse.

Skin can be oily and dehydrated at the same time. Oil describes the amount of sebum your skin produces; hydration refers to its water content. A moisturizer can help support comfort and a healthier-looking skin surface.

Know your skin

Signs your skin may need more moisture

  • Your face feels tight after cleansing.
  • Dry patches show up around your nose or cheeks.
  • Foundation or concealer clings to flaky areas.
  • Your skin feels uncomfortable after spending time in air conditioning.

What to look for

Ingredients that make a difference

01 · HYDRATE

Humectants

Glycerin and hyaluronic acid attract water to the outer layers of the skin. They can help skin feel more hydrated, especially when paired with ingredients that reduce moisture loss.

02 · SOFTEN

Emollients

Jojoba oil and squalane help soften the skin's surface and improve its feel. A formula with emollients can make rough, dry areas feel smoother.

03 · SUPPORT

Barrier ingredients

Ceramides help support the skin barrier, which helps keep moisture in and external irritants out. They are worth considering when skin feels dry or easily irritated.

Keep it simple

A summer moisturizing routine

Morning

Cleanse gently if needed, apply a light layer of moisturizer, and finish with broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher. Give each layer a moment to settle before applying the next.

After a hot day

Wash away sweat and sunscreen with a gentle cleanser. Avoid scrubbing or using very hot water. Apply moisturizer according to its directions.

Before bed

Use moisturizer again if your skin feels dry. If your cheeks are especially dry, you may prefer a slightly richer layer rather than adding several new products at once.

Common questions

Summer moisturizer, explained

Should I stop moisturizing if my skin is oily?

No. Oily skin can still become dehydrated. Try a lightweight, non-comedogenic moisturizer and adjust the amount based on how your skin feels.

Should I use a heavier cream at night?

Not necessarily. Choose a texture that leaves your skin comfortable. A richer cream may help very dry skin, while a lighter lotion may be enough for others.

Can moisturizer replace sunscreen?

A regular moisturizer does not replace broad-spectrum sunscreen. Use a sunscreen product as directed to help protect your skin from UV exposure.

When should I get professional advice?

If dryness persists, cracks, burns, or comes with a rash, consult a qualified healthcare professional or dermatologist rather than continually adding new products.
